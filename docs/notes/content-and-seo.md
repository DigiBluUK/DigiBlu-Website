## SEO

**`SITE_ORIGIN` drives every canonical, `og:image`, JSON-LD self-URL, sitemap entry and `robots.txt` line, and it is environment-overridable** - it is set in `lib/site.ts` from the `SITE_ORIGIN` variable and defaults to `https://digiblu.com` (see `.env.example`; the old "Serving from a different origin" section went with the static site), and "Branches and releases" in `platform.md` says which branch is live. Point it at a host that is not serving the files and crawlers are told a site you do not control is canonical, and no share card resolves.

**Per-engagement share cards**: each case study has its own 1200x630 card (`assets/og/case-studies/<key>.jpg`); blog and legal pages fall back to the site-wide `og-image.jpg`. `pageHead()` takes optional `ogImage`/`ogImageAlt`, and the alt text follows the image so a card naming one case study is never announced on another page.

**Favicon: the real DigiBlu mark in the light-theme ink (#0d1117) on a white rounded plate since 10 Sep 2026** - a dark disc with the db knocked out to white, the way the header logo reads in light theme - `public/assets/favicon.svg` (listed first), `favicon.png` (256, also the apple touch icon) and `public/favicon.ico` (16/32/48/256, PNG-in-ICO), all made by `scratchpad/favicon-dark.cjs` with `sharp` from the one vector trace at the same 90% fit. It was the white mark on the brand sweep from 9 Sep 2026 (asked for as "the logo with the dark blue background, as large as possible") and theme-aware before that; the sweep version was replaced on request ("not the white filled in the circle"), with the attached reference showing the dark disc on white.

An SEO audit was run and its findings prioritised into a to-do list before any of it was actioned (the user explicitly asked for research first, action second). Two items have been done; the rest were tracked in Known follow-ups, now in `qa.md` (and since superseded by the Next.js site).

- **Done since 9 to 11 Sep 2026 (every case study, legal document, service, team bio, accreditation and the contact form has its own prerendered page; see Next.js port in `platform.md` and Pages over dialogs in `consent-and-contact.md`; since 18 Sep 2026 the pages are the only place any of it appears). The original note, kept for the reasoning:** **the single-URL architecture.** Every blog post, case study, and legal page only exists as a JS-populated modal, not a separate crawlable URL, and the content only renders into the DOM on click rather than being present on load - both cap how well any of it can be individually indexed or ranked. Fixing this properly means giving each piece its own pre-rendered page, which cuts against this project's "no build tooling, no framework" identity (see the top of this file) - a real architectural decision, not a quick edit, so it's flagged rather than done unilaterally.
- **`Organization` JSON-LD**  (`<head>`). The `FAQPage` block was removed with the FAQ section - leaving schema describing content no longer on the page would have been invalid structured data. The `@graph` wrapper was flattened to the single remaining node. `Organization` carries name, legal name, URL, `logo`, `image`, description, the real registered address (`PostalAddress`, matching the footer's `CM1 1NH` — the Terms of Use said `CM1 2NH` until 10 Sep 2026, when the Terms read-through checked Companies House and corrected it to the registered office's CM1 1NH), and `sameAs` → the LinkedIn page.png` and `assets/og-image.jpg` are both `<head>`-only, so they have zero effect on the published artifact.** The rebuild script strips `<head>` entirely (Artifacts supply their own), so nothing in this SEO pass is visible on the claude.ai preview — it only matters once/if this ships to the real `digiblu.com`. Don't be surprised the artifact looks unchanged after this work; that's expected, not a bug.
  - Both images were generated via canvas in the browser preview, then decoded straight from the raw tool-result payload with Node (`Buffer.from(base64, 'base64')`) rather than hand-relayed through a written response — for `og-image.jpg` specifically, the base64 payload was long enough to overflow into a saved tool-result file, which turned out to be the safer path anyway (byte-exact, no transcription risk). Verify any future asset generated this way by checking the file's real signature and pixel dimensions (PNG: bytes 16–23 big-endian; JPEG: walk `0xFFC0`–`0xFFC3` SOF markers) before trusting it — don't assume a `toDataURL()` round-trip succeeded just because the tool call didn't error.
- **`og:image`/`twitter:image` now point at a purpose-built 1200×630 card** instead of reusing `hero.jpg` at whatever crop the browser happened to produce. Built from the real hero art (cover-cropped to the OG aspect ratio, not a new background), the same left-to-right scrim gradient the live `.hero` section uses, and the real DM Sans font loaded via `FontFace` before drawing — not a generic canvas fallback font. Added `og:image:width`/`height`/`type` and the previously-missing `twitter:title`/`description`/`image` (only `twitter:card` existed before).

## Content policy established this session

- **No fabricated named individuals.** Team = role titles, not people. This was a deliberate call after the site's public-sharing was initially blocked — the working theory was that a real company name + real address + real certifications + a non-functional lead-capture form reads exactly like a phishing page to automated review. A "Design prototype" banner plus de-attributed content were the fix at the time; sharing has worked since. (Testimonials were later replaced with real published client quotes, and **the banner and every other preview disclaimer were removed on request** when the site was finalised — see "Preview disclaimers removed" below. The no-fabricated-people rule still stands.)
  - **Real, published names are fine** — the case-study quotes name real people (e.g. Jon Willicombe at OVO, Jeremy Maynor at AssuranceSD) because those are published on digiblu.com, not invented. The rule is against *fabrication*, not attribution. Replacing invented content with verifiable client work helps the phishing-review problem rather than worsening it.
- **Real links only.** Header nav and footer links were fetched directly from the live digiblu.com (nav, services, legal pages, LinkedIn) rather than invented. In-page anchors (`#about`, `#services`, etc.) resolve to real section IDs; `scroll-behavior: smooth` with `scroll-padding-top` handles the jump.
- **The footer no longer navigates off the demo.** It originally sent visitors to the real digiblu.com in the same tab for every link, which was inconsistent with the rest of the site (blog posts, the LinkedIn icon - everything else external opens in a new tab) and meant one footer click could lose someone's place in the prototype. Now: **Company** links are in-page anchors in page order - Home, Case Studies, About Us (swapped with the 8 Sep 2026 reorder) - followed by Contact Us (Blogs was listed here until the blog was removed) (set on request; the **Services** column likewise follows the order of the six service cards, so keep both columns in sync with the page if sections or cards are reordered); **Contact Us** opens the same contact modal as the header CTA (`.js-contact-open`); **Services** links open the existing `#serviceModal` (the opener selector is `.service-learn-more, .footer-col button[data-service]` - footer buttons carry no extra class, so they inherit plain `.footer-col button` styling rather than the card "Learn more" look); **Legal** links (Terms, Privacy, Modern Slavery, Carbon Reduction Plan, Armed Forces Covenant) open `#legalModal` with the **full text** of each policy (`LEGAL_DETAILS` object) - not condensed, on explicit request. These are now real `<a href="legal/<slug>.html" data-legal="...">` (selector `.footer-col [data-legal]`, not `button[data-legal]` any more) rather than plain buttons - see Static pages (retired with the old site; in git history up to `v2.1.0`) for why and the progressive-enhancement pattern (plain click still opens the modal; since 18 Sep 2026 there is no modal and the footer's Legal links are plain links to `/legal/<slug>`). This is the one content type on the site handled differently from Blog/Case Studies: those are paraphrased because reproducing a third party's full article would be a copyright concern; a company's own legal policy text, shown on that same company's own prototype, doesn't carry that concern the way a news article does, and paraphrasing a legal document changes what it actually says. Pulled verbatim via `get_page_text` (raw DOM text, not an AI-summarized `WebFetch`, which would have paraphrased despite instructions) directly from each live page, structured by the source's own numbered sections (Terms: 18 items including intro, Privacy: 24, Modern Slavery: 8, Carbon Reduction Plan: 7, Armed Forces Covenant: 4). Numbered sub-clauses within one section are joined with a literal `\n` in the JS string (not real newlines - those aren't legal inside a single-quoted JS string) and rendered via `#legalModalList .service-modal-item p { white-space: pre-line }`, scoped to this modal only. The popup footer had a "Read the full policy on digiblu.com" link (`#legalModalReadLink`), then a `.modal-disclaimer` provenance line; **both are gone** - see "Preview disclaimers removed" below. `LEGAL_DETAILS[key].url` is still populated in the data (a provenance record for whoever maintains the policies) - don't be surprised it's unused if you go looking for where it's read. Only the LinkedIn icon still opens externally (`target="_blank" rel="noopener"`), because it always did and that's correct.
  - **All five policies are complete**, including two gaps found and closed on a follow-up pass: the Carbon Reduction Plan's "Actual tCO2e Emissions" section references two data tables that the live page renders as **images of a spreadsheet** (not real `<table>` markup or text), so `get_page_text` silently skipped them — fixed by downloading the images and transcribing the figures directly into `carbon.points[3]` (year-on-year tCO2e/employee/% savings 2022–2025, then a 2024 Scope 1/2/3 breakdown). The Privacy Policy's "Complaints" section references a "Table 1 overleaf" for complaints-handling contacts that **genuinely does not exist anywhere on the live page** (checked for table elements, images, iframes — zero) — this is a real gap in DigiBlu's own published policy, not an extraction miss, so `privacy.points[10]` restores the referencing sentence verbatim with a neutral parenthetical noting the table isn't present at time of writing, rather than fabricating contact details or silently dropping the reference.
  - **This prototype is expected to become DigiBlu's real production site**, which is the reason legal completeness was treated as a hard requirement rather than a nice-to-have — keep that bar (verbatim, complete, sourced from the live page) for anything added to `LEGAL_DETAILS` going forward, and prefer flagging a genuine source-side gap (like the missing Table 1 above) over inventing content to fill it.
  - `.footer-col a` and `.footer-col button` share one CSS rule so a visitor can't tell which mechanism a given link uses — same block layout, colour, no underline; the button reset is inert on real `<a>` tags. Verified: identical contrast ratio (6.1–6.3:1) between the two element types in both themes.
  - Desktop nav was also missing **Our Experts** (`#team`) even though the mobile menu had it — fixed earlier this session so both listed the same 7 items. **Later reduced to 5** (Home, About Us, Services, Case Studies, Our Experts) on request — Blog and FAQ were dropped from both the desktop `.nav-center` and the `.mobile-menu` (kept in sync with each other deliberately, same reasoning as the original Our Experts fix: the two nav lists should never diverge) and are now reachable only from the footer's **Company** column, which already had Blog and gained a FAQ link (`href="#faq"`) alongside it. Verified no wrap/overlap even at 901px, the narrowest width before the mobile breakpoint takes over.
    - **The nav now ends in Blogs, not FAQ.** FAQ was later re-added to the nav, then removed again along with the section itself; `Blogs` (`#blog`) took its place across the desktop nav, the mobile menu and all 20 generated pages. The footer Company column lists Blogs and no longer carries a FAQ entry.
- **The footer address reads "DigiBlu UK Limited, First Floor, Steeple House, Church Lane, Chelmsford, CM1 1NH, United Kingdom"** (9 Sep 2026, on request, in that order; it was "Digiblu UK Ltd., Steeple House, Suite 3 First Floor, Church Lane, Chelmsford, Essex, ..."). Set in `index.html`, the generator's footer and the JSON-LD `streetAddress`; the legal policies keep the wording DigiBlu published. Suite 3 and Essex were not in the address as read out and are not in the footer - restore them if DigiBlu wants them. **A second line, "Registered in England and Wales, company number 12015792.", sits under the address since 10 Sep 2026** (a `<br>` inside the same `.footer-address` paragraph, in `index.html`, the generator's footer and `components/Footer.tsx`): the Companies (Trading Disclosures) Regulations expect the registered name, number, place of registration and office on a company website, and until then the number appeared only inside the legal texts. The number is the one those texts publish.
- **Real content sourced from digiblu.com**: company description, all 6 service names/descriptions (plus each service's dedicated-page breakdown, used in the Learn More popups), all 8 case studies (client names, figures and published quotes), the blog posts (titles, dates, reading times and opening lines), NPS stat (+80 when sourced; DigiBlu has since updated it to +85, which is what the Values list shows), UK registered address, 6 certifications (added ISO 9001 and ISO 42001 — previously only 4 were listed), 5 legal page titles.
- **Article/case bodies are condensed, not copy-pasted.** Long-form source prose is paraphrased; only the short attributed client quotes and blog opening lines are verbatim. Keep it that way if you add more.
- **No em dashes in visible content**, on request — site-wide style choice, not a copyright/accuracy one. All 95 content-context em dashes (homepage copy, case studies, blog condensed points + full articles, legal policy text, including the transcribed Carbon Reduction Plan tables) were swapped for a plain hyphen (` - `, or `-` where the source already had surrounding spaces); left the 2 unspaced instances in the `no-code-low-code` article's full text (`approach—no-code`) with spaces added around the hyphen rather than a bare `approach-no-code`, which would misread as a compound word. Two occurrences used the `&mdash;` HTML entity rather than the literal `—` character (a static case-study card blurb) and needed a separate sweep — **check both forms** if you're auditing for stray em dashes later. The 25 remaining `—` in the file are all inside HTML/JS comments (developer-facing, not rendered), deliberately left alone. New content added later should stick to plain hyphens rather than reintroducing em dashes.

## Preview disclaimers removed (site finalised)

On request, **every "this is a preview / reproduced from digiblu.com" disclosure was stripped** so the site reads as a finished production website rather than a demo. Removed:

- The **`.proto-banner`** at the very top of every page ("Design prototype. This preview is not connected to a backend…") — markup gone from `index.html` and `generate-static-pages.js`, CSS rules gone from `assets/site.css`.
- All six **`.modal-disclaimer`** paragraphs: the contact form's "This is a design preview. No message is actually sent.", three "Content adapted from digiblu.com for this preview." lines (case index / case detail / service modals), the blog modal's condensed-vs-full note, and the legal modal's "Reproduced in full from the live policy on digiblu.com, which remains the current, authoritative version." The `.modal-disclaimer` CSS rule is gone too.
- The **JS that wrote the blog modal's disclaimer text** (`disclaimerEl` + the two `textContent` assignments in `showSummary()`/`showFull()`). Removing the element without these would have thrown a `TypeError` on every blog card click — worth remembering that these disclaimer paragraphs were not all static markup.
- The five **`LEGAL_DETAILS` intros** rewritten to drop the self-referential provenance ("The full Privacy Policy published on digiblu.com, reproduced by section." → "Last updated February 2024."), keeping the genuinely useful date/scope. **Terms, Privacy and Modern Slavery now read "Last updated August 2026"** (8 Sep 2026, on request; they said February 2024). **The Modern Slavery intro's "Covering the 2024 financial and calendar year" and the Carbon Reduction Plan intro's "Published as a supplier to Crown Commercial Service" were dropped on 9 Sep 2026** on team feedback: the intros are the "Last updated ..." line and nothing else (the Carbon plan keeps its January 2026 date, the Armed Forces Covenant its "Last signed 12 January 2026"). The Modern Slavery statement's body still covers the 2024 financial year and carries its 29 January 2024 signature - those are facts of the published statement, not a date stamp, and were deliberately left; DigiBlu should supply a new statement if it wants them moved. On DigiBlu's own site, citing digiblu.com as an external source read as odd anyway.

**Dangling cross-reference fixed at the same time:** the Privacy Policy's "10. Complaints" section carried a parenthetical ending "…the link below to the live policy will carry it if that changes" — but that link (`#legalModalReadLink`) had already been removed in an earlier pass, so a legal document was pointing at something that did not exist. Now reads "(Table 1 is referenced above but is not currently populated.)" **When editing legal text, grep for references to other on-page elements** — this one survived two prior passes because nothing links the prose to the element it describes.

**Superseded on 11 Sep 2026: the form posts to `/api/contact` now (see Pages over dialogs and the contact API in `consent-and-contact.md`), and it has emailed each enquiry to DigiBlu through Azure Communication Services since 14 Sep 2026; the paragraph below is the history of why the disclaimers' removal mattered.** `<form class="modal-form">` had **no backend**: `submit` calls `preventDefault()`, adds `.sent` and shows "Request sent. Thanks for reaching out. A member of the DigiBlu team will be in touch shortly to confirm a time." Nothing is transmitted, stored or emailed. With the disclaimers gone there is now **nothing telling a visitor this**, so a real prospective client can complete the form (name, work email, phone, company, message, preferred time), see a success screen, and reasonably believe DigiBlu has their enquiry. This was flagged explicitly and the trade-off accepted in order to finalise the site; the agreed condition was that it is not shared publicly until a backend is wired. ~~**Wiring that endpoint is the single highest-priority item before any public launch**~~ - done: wired on 14 Sep 2026 (see the resolved blocker in Known follow-ups in `qa.md`).
